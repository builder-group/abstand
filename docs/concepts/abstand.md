# Abstand

Most people know what they want to work on. The problem is not a lack of discipline. It is that digital environments pull at attention constantly, and every small detour adds up. By the end of the day, the work that mattered did not happen.

An Abstand is the active experience of stepping back on purpose. It begins when an Intention fires and lasts until the end condition is met or the user stops it early. The word is German for distance. That is what the app creates: intentional distance from the things pulling at attention.

"I'm taking an Abstand." "I took a 3-hour Abstand this morning." This is the moment the app is built around.

## How it is enforced

An Abstand is always created by an Intention. The Intention type determines how the distance is enforced:

- **Block**: a hard wall. The river has banks. Distractions are inaccessible.
- **Break**: a rhythm of breaks. The river pauses and recharges at intervals.
- **Flow**: progressive focus blocks. The river gathers momentum instead of being forced into a fixed timer.
- **Focus**: one clear channel. The chosen app stays visible while other apps recede.

The Abstand is the same across all Intention types: intentional distance. The type determines the mechanism.

## Portage

Ending an Abstand early is called Portage. There is no failure state. The user chose to stop, and Abstand treats that as a valid decision.
