# The River

The river is the metaphor at the core of Abstand.

Attention is like a river. It carries a fixed amount of energy. Concentrated, it moves with power and travels far. Branched across too many things (tabs, apps, notifications, context switches), it spreads thin. The user feels busy, but nothing moves.

Abstand builds the banks. The goal is not to stop the river. The goal is to give it direction. A river without banks does not disappear. It loses its shape and goes nowhere. Banks do not fight the water. They focus it.

This is why blocking works better than willpower. Willpower tries to hold the water back. Banks redirect it. The user is not fighting attention. The user is shaping where it goes.

**Block** builds hard banks: walls that keep the river in its channel for the full duration.

**Break** creates rhythm: regular moments where the river pauses and recharges before continuing.

**Flow** helps the river gather speed: short starts, adaptive focus blocks, and longer stretches when momentum is real.

**Focus** narrows the visible channel: one app stays clear while the surrounding desktop recedes.

When attention tries to escape to a blocked app or website, it branches. The branch goes nowhere. The main channel keeps flowing.
